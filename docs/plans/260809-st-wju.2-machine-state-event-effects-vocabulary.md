# machine_state, Event, and the effects vocabulary - Implementation Plan

## Overview

Stands up the three data types the whole Appendix D port is written against:
`Statifier.MachineState` (the reified interpreter position - ADR-0012
constraint 1), `Statifier.Event` (spec 5.10.1's typed event, carrying cause
metadata - constraint 4), and `Statifier.Effect` (the ADR-0003 effect
vocabulary plus the seven trace effects of `docs/observability.md`
constraint 2, with a gate that allocates nothing when tracing is off).

Nothing here steps a machine. This bead is the commitment to shape: st-wju.3
(selection), st-wju.4 (exit/entry), st-wju.5 (executable content), and
st-wju.6 (microstep/macrostep) only *emit* the vocabulary defined here, and
st-wju.7 translates it at the API boundary. Getting the shape wrong is
expensive later precisely because four beads consume it, which is why the
bead's design section asks for the representation decisions to be settled
and written into `@typedoc`/`@doc` rather than discovered per consumer.

Beads issue: st-wju.2. No research document exists; the bead's own
description, design, and acceptance criteria are the specification, read
alongside `docs/observability.md`, ADR-0003, ADR-0005, and ADR-0012.

**A note on the word "phase".** The project roadmap numbers its own phases -
Phase 1 is the core engine (this epic, `st-wju`), Phase 3 is the datamodel
(`st-af3`), Phase 4 is sessions and invoke (`st-cmq`) - and this plan numbers
its four implementation phases independently. To keep the two from being
confused, every reference below to a roadmap phase names the bead as well
("the datamodel bead, st-af3"), and a bare "Phase N" always means a phase of
*this* plan.

No human was available while this plan was written. Every question the
design section left open is settled below under "Decisions", each stated as
an assumption a reviewer may overturn. Two of them (Decision 5's counter
semantics and Decision 11's omission of a `current_event` field) are
cross-bead agreements with st-wju.6 and are recorded on that bead in
Phase 4.

## Current State Analysis

`lib/statifier/` today ends at the compiler. `Statifier.Machine` and
`lib/statifier/machine/` (`state.ex`, `transition.ex`, `content.ex`,
`block.ex`, `donedata.ex`) landed with st-wju.1 and are complete: states are
a flat document-order tuple with `index`, `parent`, self-inclusive
`index..last` descendant ranges, dense `t_index`/`c_index` identities,
retained `Location`s, and compiled `expr()` values
(`lib/statifier/machine.ex:1-259`).

What does not exist:

- No `Statifier.MachineState`, no `lib/statifier/machine_state.ex`. There is
  no runtime state type at all - `grep` finds no `machine_state` anywhere in
  `lib/` or `test/`.
- No `Statifier.Event`. Events exist only as corpus-harness dialect (a map
  with a `"name"` key) inside `test/support/case.ex`, which st-wju.7 will
  adapt.
- No `Statifier.Effect`, no effect type of any kind. ADR-0003's
  `{:send, ...}` sketch has never been spelled out in code.
- `docs/observability.md:146-165`'s implementation checklist has its three
  compiler items ticked (st-wju.1) and its trace/machine_state/cause items
  still open. This bead ticks the first, third, and fourth of the remaining
  four; the second (`microstep` exists, macrostep folds over it) is
  st-wju.6's.

Constraints discovered that bound the design:

- **The gate's coverage floor is 90% of `lib/` only**
  (`docs/testing.md:236-242`). A phase that adds a module must add its tests
  in the same phase or it lowers the floor - this is the main force behind
  the phase split below.
- **Every new test asserting `lib/` behavior needs a sabotage line**
  (`docs/testing.md:78-134`), format `# sabotage: <mutation> -> red`, above
  the `test` line and above any `@tag`.
- **Predicates take Elixir's `?` form** per ADR-0002's 2026-08-09 amendment;
  `Credo.Check.Readability.PredicateFunctionNames` runs `strict: true` and is
  not agent-editable (ADR-0011). `trace?/1` here, never `is_trace/1`.
- **Existing house style in `lib/statifier/machine*`**: `@enforce_keys` for
  fields with no sensible default, `@type t ::` spelled out field by field, a
  moduledoc table for kind-scoped fields, and `@doc`s that name the Appendix
  D counterpart. The new modules match it.
- **Test style**: `test/statifier/machine_test.exs:1-60` builds its fixtures
  by running a triple-quoted XML heredoc (4-space base indent) through
  `Parser -> Lowering -> Validator -> Compiler` and pins an `@indexes` map so
  assertions read in state ids. Phase 2 and Phase 4 reuse that exact helper
  shape rather than hand-constructing `%Machine{}` literals.

## Desired End State

Three modules exist, fully documented, fully tested, and consumed by nothing
yet:

1. `Statifier.Event` + `Statifier.Event.Cause` - name, data, spec-5.10.1
   `type`, and a cause slot that is `nil` for anything the platform did not
   raise itself.
2. `Statifier.MachineState` - machine, full configuration, FIFO internal
   queue, history values, datamodel slot, `running`, `status`, `macrostep`,
   `microstep`, `trace`; a `new/2` constructor; the queue and counter
   operations that are the *only* writers of those fields; and the derived
   active-leaf-states view.
3. `Statifier.Effect` - one `@type t()` union covering six core effects and
   seven trace effects, the trace payload structs under
   `Statifier.Effect.Trace.*`, `trace?/1`, and the `trace/3` emission gate.

Verification that the end state is reached: `mix quality` is green with
coverage at or above the floor; `test/statifier/machine_state_acceptance_test.exs`
asserts every line of the bead's acceptance criteria that a test can decide
(Phase 4); and `docs/observability.md`'s checklist has three more boxes
ticked with the module that satisfies each.

### Key Discoveries:

- `lib/statifier/machine.ex:96-115` - `at/2`, `transition/2`, `content/2` are
  the index readers `MachineState` and the trace effects lean on; trace
  effects carry indexes, and tooling calls these to resolve them. No trace
  effect ever embeds a `%Machine.State{}`.
- `lib/statifier/machine.ex:154-158` - `atomic?/2` is `children == []`, and a
  `:final` is atomic. That is exactly the predicate the active-leaf-states
  view needs; the view is a filter, not a new traversal.
- `lib/statifier/machine.ex:80-89` - `%Machine{}` already carries `binding`,
  so Appendix D's `binding` global is reconstructible from
  `machine_state.machine` and needs no field of its own (constraint 1 is
  satisfied by reconstructibility, not by duplication).
- `docs/observability.md:69-76` - the three trace rules: gated by an option,
  carrying counters and constraint-3 identities, and ordinary members of the
  effect list. All three are testable, and Phase 3 tests each.
- `docs/datamodel.md:33-41` - the evaluation context is built once per
  macrostep and is a predicator context, i.e. a map. The datamodel slot is
  therefore shaped as a map today, so st-af3 adds content and not shape.
- ADR-0003 (`docs/adr/0003-pure-core-with-effects.md:17-22`) writes effects
  as tagged tuples; the bead's design section explicitly permits structs for
  the trace effects. Decision 3 reconciles the two.
- `test/statifier/machine_test.exs:11-19` - the `compile!/1` helper the new
  tests copy.

## What We're NOT Doing

- **No interpreter.** No `microstep`, no `select_transitions`, no
  `enter_states`, no block runner. Those are st-wju.3 through st-wju.6. This
  bead emits no effect from any code path - it only defines what an effect
  is and provides the gate other beads emit through.
- **No predicates that read the machine.** `in_final_state?`, `atomic?`,
  `descendant?` and friends belong to `Statifier.Machine` (already there) or
  to st-wju.3/4. The single exception is `active_leaf_states/1`, which the
  bead names explicitly as this bead's derived view.
- **No `states_to_invoke` field.** Appendix D carries it as a global, but
  every read and write of it is in the invoke passes of `main_event_loop`,
  which are the invoke bead, st-cmq. Adding it now repeats exactly the mistake the
  bead warns about for `origin`/`sendid`: a dead field nobody can test. The
  omission is documented in `MachineState`'s moduledoc so st-cmq adds it
  deliberately rather than rediscovering the need.
- **No `origin` / `sendid` on `Event`.** The bead says leave them out rather
  than dead; the send/invoke bead (st-cmq) adds them.
- **No external event queue in `MachineState`** - Decision 8. This is a
  deliberate, documented divergence from the pseudocode's globals.
- **No string ids anywhere.** Configuration, history values, and every trace
  identity are integer indexes (ADR-0005). Translation to ids is st-wju.7's
  API boundary and nowhere deeper.
- **No effect interpreter, no telemetry, no session.** `Statifier.Session` is
  the session work of st-cmq; nothing here knows what an effect list is
  eventually *for*.
- **No mechanical guard against direct counter writes.** Making
  `begin_macrostep/1` and `begin_microstep/1` the only advance sites is
  enforced by their existence plus review, as the bead says. A test that
  greps `lib/` for `macrostep:` assignments was considered and declined: it
  would fire on the constructor and on any future legitimate rebuild, and a
  guard that has to be exempted is worse than the review item it replaces.
- **No performance benchmark of the trace gate.** The gate's laziness is
  asserted behaviorally (this plan's Phase 3), not measured. `docs/observability.md:144`
  puts performance work on tracing out of scope.

## Implementation Approach

Four phases, split on module boundaries, in dependency order: `Event` has no
dependencies, `MachineState` holds a queue of `Event`s, `Effect`'s trace
payloads are stamped from a `MachineState`, and the last phase asserts the
bead's acceptance criteria end to end and updates the docs. Each phase adds
one module (or one acceptance-test module plus docs) together with its own
tests, so each is independently committable with a green full gate and none
of them leaves an untested module behind to sink coverage.

The phases do not parallelize across worktrees - they are strictly
sequential within one small bead - which is fine: `docs/workflow.md`'s
parallelism is across beads, not within one.

Every design question the bead left open is settled below before any code is
written, because four downstream beads read these shapes and a shape decided
mid-implementation is a shape decided twice.

## Decisions

**Decision 1 - module and file names.** `Statifier.MachineState` in
`lib/statifier/machine_state.ex`; `Statifier.Event` and
`Statifier.Event.Cause` in `lib/statifier/event.ex` and
`lib/statifier/event/cause.ex`; `Statifier.Effect` in
`lib/statifier/effect.ex` with the trace payloads under
`lib/statifier/effect/trace/`.

`Statifier.MachineState` reads one character away from
`Statifier.Machine.State`, which is a *compiled state*, not a runtime
position. The names stay anyway: `machine_state` is the term ADR-0012,
`docs/observability.md`, and every sibling bead use, and renaming the concept
to avoid a lexical near-collision would cost more than the collision does.
The mitigation is a convention stated in both moduledocs - interpreter
modules alias `Statifier.MachineState` as `MachineState` and
`Statifier.Machine.State` as `State`, never abbreviating either further -
plus a cross-reference line in each moduledoc naming the other.

**Decision 2 - the internal queue is `:queue`.** Erlang's `:queue` gives
amortized O(1) FIFO with no hand-rolled front/rear pair to get wrong, and
`:queue.queue(Event.t())` is a spec dialyzer accepts. Its cost is that the
representation is not directly readable, which collides with constraint 1's
"complete, inspectable" position, so `MachineState.internal_events/1`
returns the pending events as a plain list in FIFO order and is the
inspection and assertion path for every test and every future debugger. No
code outside `MachineState` touches `:queue` directly.

One consequence must be written down here because st-wju.6 depends on it:
two `:queue` values holding the same events in the same order can differ
structurally (the front/rear split depends on the push/pop history), so
`==` on two `%MachineState{}` values is *not* a reliable "same position"
test. st-wju.6's constraint-1 acceptance test (fold-to-quiescence versus
step-by-step yields the same result) must compare a normalized view -
configuration, `internal_events/1`, history values, counters, status - not
raw struct equality. This is recorded in `MachineState`'s moduledoc and on
st-wju.6 in Phase 4.

**Decision 3 - every effect is `{tag, payload_struct}`.** ADR-0003 spells
effects as tagged tuples (`{:send, ...}`, `{:cancel, id}`); the bead's design
section wants structs for trace effects because they carry many fields and
tooling pattern-matches on the struct name. Both are satisfied by one rule:
an effect is a two-element tuple whose first element is the vocabulary tag
and whose second is a struct with named fields.

```elixir
{:log, %Statifier.Effect.Log{label: "tick", value: nil, c_index: 7, macrostep: 2, microstep: 1}}
{:trace, %Statifier.Effect.Trace.EntrySet{indexes: [1, 2], macrostep: 2, microstep: 1}}
```

The tag keeps `case effect do {:log, log} -> ...` reading the way ADR-0003
writes it and makes "is this a trace effect?" a single match
(`Effect.trace?/1`), which st-wju.6's "trace-off produces zero trace effects"
assertion needs. The struct payload gives named fields, dialyzer coverage,
and room to add fields in st-cmq without changing any arity. Positional
tuples with four or five elements were rejected: `{:send, target, event,
data, send_id}` is unreadable at the call site and a field added later
breaks every match.

**Decision 4 - the trace gate is `Effect.trace/3`, a macro over one `if`.**

```elixir
require Statifier.Effect, as: Effect

Effect.trace(machine_state, Effect.Trace.EntrySet, indexes: entry_order)
```

expands to

```elixir
if machine_state.trace do
  [{:trace, Statifier.Effect.Trace.EntrySet.new(machine_state, indexes: entry_order)}]
else
  []
end
```

Because `if` is itself a macro, the payload expression is only evaluated in
the taken branch: trace-off is one boolean field read plus an empty list,
with no struct built and no argument computed. Every trace payload module
defines `new/2` taking the machine_state (from which it stamps `macrostep`
and `microstep`) and a keyword list of its own fields, so no call site ever
repeats the counters and no call site can forget them.

**Decision 5 - counter semantics (agreed with st-wju.6).**

- `new/2` sets `macrostep: 0, microstep: 0`. Zero means "no macrostep has
  begun"; it is never the number of a real step.
- `begin_macrostep/1` is the only writer of `macrostep`: it increments it by
  one and resets `microstep` to 0. st-wju.6 calls it once in `initialize/2`
  (so the initialization macrostep is **macrostep 1**) and once per accepted
  external event in `handle_event/2` (so the first external event is
  **macrostep 2**).
- `begin_microstep/1` is the only writer of `microstep`: it increments it by
  one. It is called once per *pseudocode microstep* - one exit / execute /
  enter round - so the first microstep of a macrostep is **microstep 1**.
- A selection round that dequeues an internal event enabling no transitions
  does **not** advance `microstep`: no exit or entry happened, so there was
  no microstep. The consumed event is still visible, because the
  event-dequeued trace effect is emitted at the current counters. This is
  st-wju.6's own recommendation, adopted here so the two writeups cannot
  disagree.
- Cause metadata and trace effects are both stamped with the counters *as
  they stand at the moment of the stamp*, i.e. after the `begin_*` call for
  the step they belong to.

**Decision 6 - `running` and `status` are both present and mean different
things.** `running` is Appendix D's `running` flag verbatim: the interpreter
loop's continue condition, set false when a top-level final is entered.
`status` is `:running | :done` and becomes `:done` only after
`exit_interpreter` has finished and the terminal `{:done, _}` effect has been
produced. They therefore differ for exactly the window between top-level
final entry and the end of `exit_interpreter`, which is the window in which
`exit_interpreter` still has to run onexit content on a machine that is no
longer running. `new/2` produces `running: true, status: :running`; a
machine_state that has not been initialized yet is indistinguishable from a
running one, which is harmless because `new/2` is only ever called by
st-wju.6's `initialize/2`, immediately before it enters the initial states.

**Decision 7 - the datamodel slot is a map, defaulting to `%{}`.**
`docs/datamodel.md:33-41` says the evaluation context is a predicator context
built once per macrostep, and a predicator context is a map. Typing the slot
`@type datamodel :: map()` today means st-af3 adds content, not shape, which
is what the bead asks for. It is *not* typed `term()`/`any()`: an opaque
`nil` would let st-af3 change the shape silently and would give dialyzer
nothing to check.

**Decision 8 - the external queue is not in `MachineState`, and the
divergence is documented for st-wju.6.** Appendix D's `main_event_loop` owns
both queues; the pure core here takes one external event per call (ADR-0003),
and the session owns external queueing (st-cmq). This is a *mechanical*
deviation in ADR-0002's sense - the semantics of processing one external
event are unchanged, only the storage of the waiting ones moves outward - so
it is legal, but it must carry its reason at the port site. This plan's
Phase 4 writes
the divergence into `MachineState`'s moduledoc and records it on st-wju.6, so
the `main_event_loop` port comes with the comment ADR-0002 requires rather
than inventing the justification then.

**Decision 9 - history values are keyed by history-state index.**
`%{optional(non_neg_integer()) => MapSet.t(non_neg_integer())}`: the key is
the `:history` pseudo-state's own index, the value is the recorded set of
state indexes (immediate active children for shallow, active atomic
descendants for deep - st-wju.4 decides what goes in; this bead only fixes
the container). Default `%{}`. Absence of a key is the "never recorded" case
st-wju.4 tests for, so `%{}` and a key mapping to an empty MapSet are
deliberately different.

**Decision 10 - `Event.data` defaults to `nil`, and platform events ride the
internal queue.** `nil` is "no data", distinct from `%{}` ("data, empty"),
which the datamodel bead (st-af3) will need. `type` is `:external | :internal |
:platform` per spec 5.10.1; the `@doc` records that `:platform` events
(`error.execution`, `error.communication`) are enqueued on the *internal*
queue exactly like `:internal` ones - the type describes provenance, not the
queue - so st-wju.5 and st-af3 do not have to rediscover it.

**Decision 11 - there is no `current_event` field, and here is why.**
`docs/observability.md:28-31` names "current event, internal queue position"
as the loop state that must not live on the call stack, so the omission needs
a justification rather than a shrug. The justification is that the microstep
boundary, as st-wju.6 defines it, is *after* a complete exit / execute /
enter round, and at that boundary no event is in flight: each microstep
selects its own transition set (eventless first, else dequeue one internal
event) and consumes it within that same round. The internal queue position is
reified - it is the queue itself. The external event handed to
`handle_event/2` is consumed by the first selection round of that macrostep
and is not held across a boundary.

The consequence to accept: this fixes the pause granularity at whole
microsteps. A future debugger that wants to pause *between* select and
enter would need a field for the in-flight event. That is additive - a new
field with a default breaks no caller - and st-wju.6 may add it if its port
finds it needs one. Recorded on st-wju.6 in Phase 4 so the decision is
visible when `microstep/1` is actually written, not just here. (`_event`, the
spec 5.10 system variable holding the last processed event, is datamodel
content and lands with st-af3 in the datamodel slot; it is not this field.)

**Decision 12 - `trace` is a boolean.** `trace: false` by default, typed
`@type trace :: boolean()`. `docs/observability.md:70` gives `trace: true` as
the working name and the bead allows a level later. If a level arrives, it
arrives as a separate field and `trace` stays the precomputed boolean the
gate reads, because the gate's contract - one field read, no comparison, no
function call - is what makes the untraced path free. This is stated in the
`@typedoc` so a later level does not turn the gate into `level != :none`.

**Decision 13 - the seven trace payloads and their fields.** One module each
under `Statifier.Effect.Trace`, every one carrying `macrostep` and
`microstep`, every identity an index:

| Module | Vocabulary point | Fields beyond the counters |
|---|---|---|
| `EventDequeued` | an external or internal event is selected for processing | `event` (`Event.t()`), `from` (`:external \| :internal`) |
| `TransitionsSelected` | `select_transitions` returned, including the empty set | `t_indexes` (`[non_neg_integer()]`), `event` (`Event.t() \| nil`, `nil` for an eventless round) |
| `ExitSet` | `compute_exit_set` result, before exiting | `indexes` (`[non_neg_integer()]`, exit order) |
| `ContentExecuted` | a block of executable content ran | `owner` (`{:onentry, state_index, ordinal} \| {:onexit, state_index, ordinal} \| {:transition, t_index}`), `c_indexes` |
| `EntrySet` | `compute_entry_set` result, before entering | `indexes` (`[non_neg_integer()]`, entry order) |
| `MacrostepStable` | the configuration reached quiescence | `configuration` (`MapSet.t(non_neg_integer())`) |
| `Done` | top-level final entry / `exit_interpreter` | `donedata` (`term() \| nil`), `configuration` (the configuration as it stood at exit) |

`owner` on `ContentExecuted` carries an ordinal because a state may write
several `<onentry>` elements and `Machine.State.onentry` is a list of
`Block.t()` with no index of its own; the ordinal is the block's position in
that list. Downstream beads may add fields to these structs; they may not
remove the counters, and they may not replace an index with a struct
(`docs/observability.md:73-74`).

**Decision 14 - the six core effects.** `{:send, %Effect.Send{}}`,
`{:send_delayed, %Effect.SendDelayed{}}`, `{:cancel, %Effect.Cancel{}}`,
`{:invoke, %Effect.Invoke{}}`, `{:done, %Effect.Done{}}`,
`{:log, %Effect.Log{}}` - exactly the ADR-0003 set the bead names, no more.
Each payload carries the counters and, where it has one, the `c_index` of the
content node that produced it, so a session-side subscriber can correlate
without a side channel. The core-engine epic only ever produces `:log` and
`:done`; the other four are defined and unproduced, which is the point of
this bead. Their fields are the spec's own attribute names
(`send`: `event`, `target`, `type`, `data`, `send_id`; `send_delayed` adds
`delay_ms`; `cancel`: `send_id`; `invoke`: `invoke_id`, `type`, `src`,
`params`, `autoforward`, `source` state index). st-cmq owns their semantics
and may add fields.

## Phase 1: Defines the Event struct and its cause metadata

### Overview

`Statifier.Event` and `Statifier.Event.Cause`: the value every queue holds
and every selection round matches against, with the constraint-4 cause slot
that lets the datamodel bead's (st-af3) `error.execution` say which content
node raised it
without a global lookup.

### Changes Required:

#### 1. The cause struct

**File**: `lib/statifier/event/cause.ex` (new)
**Changes**: Where an internally raised event came from, and when.

```elixir
defmodule Statifier.Event.Cause do
  @moduledoc """
  Why an internally raised event exists - `docs/observability.md` constraint
  4. `origin` is a constraint-3 identity (a `t_index` or a `c_index`, never
  a struct); `macrostep`/`microstep` are the counters as they stood when the
  event was raised, per `Statifier.MachineState`'s counter contract.

  Cause travels *with* the event so a consumer - the first of which is
  st-af3's `error.execution` message - resolves it through
  `Statifier.Machine.transition/2` or `content/2` and the retained
  `Location`, with no global lookup and no ambient step context.
  """

  @enforce_keys [:origin, :macrostep, :microstep]
  defstruct [:origin, :macrostep, :microstep]

  @type origin :: {:transition, non_neg_integer()} | {:content, non_neg_integer()}

  @type t :: %__MODULE__{
          origin: origin(),
          macrostep: non_neg_integer(),
          microstep: non_neg_integer()
        }

  @spec new(origin(), non_neg_integer(), non_neg_integer()) :: t()
  def new(origin, macrostep, microstep)
end
```

#### 2. The event struct

**File**: `lib/statifier/event.ex` (new)
**Changes**: name, data, spec 5.10.1 `type`, nilable `cause`, plus one
constructor per type. `@enforce_keys [:name, :type]`; `data: nil`,
`cause: nil`.

```elixir
@type type :: :external | :internal | :platform

@type t :: %__MODULE__{
        name: String.t(),
        data: term(),
        type: type(),
        cause: Cause.t() | nil
      }

@spec external(String.t(), keyword()) :: t()   # data: opt; cause is always nil
@spec internal(String.t(), Cause.t(), keyword()) :: t()
@spec platform(String.t(), Cause.t(), keyword()) :: t()
```

The moduledoc records Decision 10 (`:platform` events ride the internal
queue; `type` is provenance, not routing), Decision 11's note that `_event`
is datamodel content rather than a field here, and that `origin`/`sendid`
are deliberately absent until st-cmq.

#### 3. Tests

**File**: `test/statifier/event_test.exs`, `test/statifier/event/cause_test.exs` (new)
**Changes**: constructors set the right `type`; `external/2` leaves `cause`
`nil`; `internal/3` and `platform/3` carry the cause through unchanged;
`data` defaults to `nil` and is distinguishable from `%{}`; `Cause.new/3`
accepts both origin shapes. Each test carries its sabotage line - e.g.
`# sabotage: Event.internal/3 stamps :external -> red`.

#### 3. The union, the predicate, and the gate

**File**: `lib/statifier/effect.ex`
**Changes**:

```elixir
@type core ::
        {:send, Send.t()}
        | {:send_delayed, SendDelayed.t()}
        | {:cancel, Cancel.t()}
        | {:invoke, Invoke.t()}
        | {:done, Done.t()}
        | {:log, Log.t()}

@type trace ::
        {:trace, Trace.EventDequeued.t()}
        | {:trace, Trace.TransitionsSelected.t()}
        | ...

@type t :: core() | trace()

@spec trace?(t()) :: boolean()
def trace?({:trace, _payload}), do: true
def trace?(_effect), do: false

defmacro trace(machine_state, payload_module, fields) do
  quote do
    machine_state = unquote(machine_state)

    if machine_state.trace do
      [{:trace, unquote(payload_module).new(machine_state, unquote(fields))}]
    else
      []
    end
  end
end
```

The `machine_state` argument is bound to a variable inside the `quote`
*before* the `if`, so a call site passing an expression
(`Effect.trace(begin_microstep(ms), ...)`) evaluates it exactly once. Writing
`unquote(machine_state)` twice - once for `.trace` and once as `new/2`'s
argument - would double-evaluate it, which is the bug this shape exists to
avoid. `fields`, by contrast, is deliberately spliced inside the `if`'s
`do` branch only: that is what makes the off-path build nothing.

The moduledoc is the single source of truth the bead asks for: the whole
vocabulary in one table, the `{tag, payload}` rule (Decision 3), the gate's
contract (Decision 4 - one field read, nothing built when off), the rule that
trace effects carry indexes and counters and never a `%Machine.State{}`
(`docs/observability.md:73-74`), and the rule that trace effects are ordinary
members of the same list with the same ordering, never a side channel.

#### 4. Tests

**File**: `test/statifier/effect_test.exs` and
`test/statifier/effect/trace_test.exs` (new)
**Changes**:

- Each of the seven trace payloads: `new/2` stamps `macrostep`/`microstep`
  from the machine_state and sets its own fields.
- `trace?/1` is true for every `{:trace, _}` and false for each of the six
  core tags (table-driven over the vocabulary, so a seventh trace point added
  without a case fails).
- Gate on: `Effect.trace(ms_on, Trace.EntrySet, indexes: [1, 2])` returns a
  one-element list holding the stamped struct.
- Gate off: returns `[]`.
- **Gate laziness**: with `trace: false`, a call whose `fields` expression
  sends a message to `self()` produces no message - the payload expression
  is never evaluated, which is what "allocates nothing when off" means in a
  form a test can decide. With `trace: true`, the message arrives (so the
  test cannot pass by the expression being unreachable for another reason).
- **Gate single-evaluation**: the `machine_state` argument is evaluated
  exactly once. Pass an expression with an observable side effect
  (`send(self(), :evaluated) && ms`) as the first argument, with `trace: true`
  and again with `trace: false`, and assert exactly one message each time -
  the assertion a doubly-spliced `unquote` fails on the trace-on run.
- Sabotage lines throughout - e.g.
  `# sabotage: Effect.trace/3 builds the payload before the if -> red` for
  the laziness test, `# sabotage: trace?/1 returns true for {:log, _} -> red`.

## Performance Considerations

- **The trace gate is the only hot-path cost this bead adds**, and it is one
  boolean field read per emission site when off (Decision 4). The laziness
  test is the guard; there is no benchmark, per
  `docs/observability.md:144`.
- **`:queue` is amortized O(1)** for both ends; the naive
  `list ++ [event]` alternative is O(n) per raise and would show up on
  documents that raise in a loop. `internal_events/1` is O(n) and is an
  inspection path only - no interpreter code path may call it.
- **The configuration is a `MapSet` of integers**, so membership and
  set operations in st-wju.3/4 are the integer comparisons ADR-0005 was
  adopted for. `active_leaf_states/1` is O(n) in the configuration size and
  is called at the API boundary (st-wju.7), not inside the loop; its `@doc`
  says so, so nobody puts it in a per-microstep path.
- Trace payload structs are built only when tracing is on, so the traced path
  allocates thirteen-ish small structs per macrostep and the untraced path
  allocates none.

## Appendix D Deviations

Per ADR-0002 and `.claude/wurk/plan.md`'s Appendix D rule, this bead ports no
pseudocode function - it defines the data the ports will run on - but it
makes two structural choices that a later reader could mistake for semantic
deviations. Both are mechanical, and both are recorded where the port that
inherits them will be written:

1. **Loop state moves onto a struct.** The pseudocode holds the internal
   queue, the current event, and the counters in globals and loop locals.
   Storing them on `%MachineState{}` changes storage, not semantics, and is
   the deviation `docs/observability.md:44-45` sanctions. st-wju.6's
   `main_event_loop` port carries the inline comment.
2. **The external queue leaves the core** (Decision 8). One external event
   per call (ADR-0003); the session owns queueing. The semantics of
   processing a given external event are identical; only the storage of the
   waiting ones moves outward. st-wju.6's port carries the comment naming
   ADR-0003.

Neither is a licence for a semantic change: nothing here alters which
transitions are selected, in what order states are exited or entered, or what
events are raised.

## Open Questions

None outstanding. Every question the bead's design section raised is settled
above as a numbered Decision. Three of them are assumptions a human may
overturn without invalidating the phase structure, listed here so a reviewer
can find them quickly:

1. **Decision 3** - `{tag, payload_struct}` for *every* effect, including the
   trace ones, rather than bare structs for trace and positional tuples for
   core. If a reviewer prefers bare trace structs, `trace?/1` becomes a
   module-name check and nothing else in the plan moves.
2. **Decision 5** - the initialization macrostep is macrostep 1 (not 0), and
   `microstep` counts exit/enter rounds rather than selection rounds. This is
   an agreement with st-wju.6, recorded on that bead in Phase 4; changing it
   means changing both writeups together.
3. **Decision 11** - no `current_event` field, on the argument that no event
   is in flight at a microstep boundary. This is the one decision that could
   need revisiting inside st-wju.6 rather than before it; the field is
   additive if so, and the reasoning is recorded on that bead.
